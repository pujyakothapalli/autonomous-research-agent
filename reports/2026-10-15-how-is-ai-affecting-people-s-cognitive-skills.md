## Summary

AI use is associated with measurable declines in critical thinking and cognitive engagement, particularly among younger users. Research indicates that reliance on AI systems may shift thinking from active analysis to passive consumption, and that switching from AI back to independent work produces detectable changes in brain activity and cognitive performance. However, the research base remains limited, with most findings drawn from small studies or recent 2025 research.

## Findings

**Prevalence of AI Use in Education**

86% of students now use AI in their studies, and 56% have used AI on assignments or exams [6].

**Cognitive Engagement and Brain Activity**

An MIT study of 54 American adults (ages 18–39) found that ChatGPT users showed lower electrical brain connectivity than search engine users and those using only their brains for the first three essays [2]. When participants switched from ChatGPT to independent work, they demonstrated "much lower electrical connectivity in their brains than the brain-only group's third session" [2].

A Stanford study found that ChatGPT assistance led to significantly lower cognitive engagement scores compared to a control group without AI assistance [5].

**Memory and Ownership**

The MIT study found that participants who switched from ChatGPT to independent work showed decreased perception of ownership of their writing and lower recall of quotes from essays they had written [2].

**Cognitive Offloading**

Overreliance on AI automation risks diminishing critical thinking, memory retention, and motivation [1]. When learners let AI handle analysis or writing, they engage less deeply with material, weakening cognitive growth [1]. Cognitive offloading—a reduced need for independent thinking due to reliance on automation—may result from AI use [2].

A 2025 study suggests AI use may shift critical thinking in three ways: recall and comprehension from information gathering to information verification; application from problem-solving to AI response integration; and analysis, synthesis, and evaluation from task execution to task stewardship [2].

**Age and Critical Thinking**

Emerging research suggests that increased AI use may negatively affect critical thinking skills [2]. A 2025 study found that those who use AI more—potentially ages 17 to 25—may have reduced critical thinking abilities [2].

**AI's Unique Risks**

Unlike calculators, which perform deterministic mathematical operations, AI systems generate responses through pattern matching, often producing plausible-sounding but incorrect information [6]. Students can input complete assignment prompts and receive fully formed responses without understanding the subject matter, assignment requirements, or output quality [6].

## Contradictions and Open Questions

No contradictions were found in the verified claims.

**Open questions include:**
- Whether observed cognitive changes are temporary or persistent
- Whether effects vary significantly across age groups beyond the 17–25 range studied
- Whether certain types of AI use (e.g., supplementary vs. primary) produce different outcomes
- Long-term implications of reduced brain connectivity during AI-assisted tasks
